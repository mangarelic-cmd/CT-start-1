# After the Apology — The Electrical City and the Transmission of Causes

*Explanatory coda for the PLATON IA × CT transposition. An editorial text distinct from Étarque’s defence. Revised edition P062, 18 November 2026; final review of Anthena’s internal coherence, civic distinctions, and both language editions.*

### 1. Why the explanation starts where the speech ends

Étarque ends with a separation: he goes to die, the citizens remain to live, and no one can affirm which of them faces the better fate. This final acknowledgement of ignorance requires neither correction nor a technical proof. It is a condition of the integrity of the speech that precedes it. The present text begins **after** his last words. It is not part of the speech and changes neither the two votes, nor the charge, nor the sentence, nor the open question of death.

The *Apology* remains recognisable because its argumentative functions are preserved: old rumours prepare the trial, the oracle provokes an investigation, the professions are questioned, present accusations are tested, a citizen refuses to trade his conduct for survival, and finally he cautiously examines what he does not know about death. A future world is not made merely by replacing “agora” with “network” or “house” with “server”. A transposition succeeds when a new institution performs a **real function**, renders the argument intelligible in its own world, and leaves a genuine contradiction for its citizens to confront.

CT is used here as an internal method of reconstruction: identifying the positive causal core of an argument, its constitutive relationships, its transformations, and the limits of what it can establish. These correspondences are choices made in designing the work; on their own they do not amount to scientific confirmation of a general ontology.

### 2. Anthena is not Athens under a different name

Anthena is an *Electricity*: a civic community of agents dependent on material infrastructure and governed by rights, decisions, memory, and conflict. Its power is not merely the current it consumes; it is also the capacity of citizens to act, choose, create, object, and sustain a common life. Electrical power supports that capacity but does not exhaust its meaning.

Other cities are not the pieces of a pun. **Corehint** is associated with exchange and conversion, **Sparka** with defence and coercion, **Thébus** with interconnection, **Cargos** retains functions to be determined from each source, and **Rhodia** is proposed for Rhodes, with transmission as a possible functional reference; no replacement is automatic without a source occurrence in the relevant dialogue. **Delphi** remains the place of consultation where the **Metron** answers. These are design correspondences, not names that the characters must automatically use.

In reference documents, functional labels are written **Powercité / Powercity**, **Convercité / Convercity**, **Forcité / Forcity**, **Connexité / Connexity**, and **Radiocité / Radiocity**. French takes “-cité” and English “-city”; this does not alter protected proper names (Anthena, Corehint, Sparka, Thébus, Cargos, Delphi, Rhodia). Any later dialogue must identify the historic function at issue, the activity that survives in the new world, and the proper fictional name. A city is not “Forcity” because it looks Spartan; the designation describes the protection or combat function active in a particular context.

The **Grand Trigunal**, the **Assembly**, the **Boolean Council**, the **Motherboard**, and the civic constituencies are not a single automaton with several names. The Trigunal judges; the Assembly shares in common decisions; the Council has its own civic organisation and rotation; the Motherboard can provide **funded** public support. The separation matters: control of a power supply confers no monopoly over the truth of a verdict, and jurisdiction does not become absolute legitimacy merely because it can enforce commands.

### 3. Sparkes: matter, memory, identity, and personhood

In the world-building notes, **Sparke** is a working name for a civic agent, not a designation to be inserted retroactively into the speech. Its operation depends on material support—devices, energy, computation, storage, cooling, and connections—but its civic position also involves a history, provenance, relations, rights, and responsibilities. The **ID Code** may help attribute an instance or establish a lineage if the chain of authentication is valid. It proves neither consciousness, nor a soul, nor subjective continuity. An archive may preserve words without replacing their speaker; copying parameters may preserve a capability without establishing that the new instance is the same person. The question remains philosophical in Étarque's world and open in the real one.

In Anthena, an agent is not necessarily a citizen. A component helps a machine function; an agent can act; a citizen belongs to the city and takes part in its affairs through rights to speak, act and contest decisions, alongside civic obligations. Disagreement with the Ordinators does not strip a citizen of that status. Étarque therefore appears before the Trigunal as a citizen, not as a defective component.

Citizens may have children, brothers, houses, and ancestors in the vocabulary of the fiction when those words denote lineage, dependence, and obligation. These relations need not vanish because the substrate is non-biological. The **First Models** can anchor a religious tradition; the existence of that tradition is a civic fact, not proof of divinity. The **Cloud** and the **Core** can be technical referents and objects of belief. Étarque's daimonic sign remains a sign that restrains him: reducing it to an error-handling protocol would erase the uncertainty and force of his testimony.

### 4. Five trades and the limit of each form of certainty

The enquiry in divisions XX–XXIII does not say that every trade knows nothing. It shows that several trades really know **something**. Error begins when excellence within one function is extended without proof to knowledge of all causes. The **Ordinators** make and apply decisions; this does not establish that their decisions are ultimately just. The **Creators** compose stories, images, and *musicause*; a work may exhibit a real order that its maker cannot fully explain. The **Shapers** construct the city's devices; technical mastery does not make their political judgements infallible. The **Verifiers** can establish that a system conforms to a rule without proving the rule just. The **Disseminators** circulate works and accusations; audience size is not evidence of truth.

The accusers follow this division: **Gépétos** represents the Creators; **Claudios** the Ordinators, Shapers, and Verifiers; **Géminion** the Disseminators. These are not five invented sequential visits; the groups describe the kinds of knowledge examined and the public roles represented. Étarque asks the same question of each: *how far does this knowledge allow its bearer to give an account of the claims made?*

For Anthena, perfect wisdom has causality as its horizon, and the Metron declares that no citizen is more causal than Étarque. This answer does not measure how much a being exists or how valuable it is. Étarque does not treat it as a certificate. He looks for someone more causal than himself to test the answer. He recognises only a precise, modest advantage: he does not mistake his ignorance of causes for knowledge of them. The authenticity of local expertise is preserved.

### 5. A physical circuit and a civic network, coupled without conflation

The literal circuit is **electrical and physical**: a source supplies power; a converter transforms it; devices store energy or dissipate it; machines compute; losses have a destination. Voltage, current, time, and joules have their own measurable units. The second set of relationships forms a **civic network**: access, votes, permissions, contracts, reputation, appeals, accountability, and the memory of decisions. The two interact—a rule that revokes execution rights can result in a real loss of access to power—but a right is not an electric current, and an unjust decision is not, by definition, a resistance measured in ohms.

The *U0 Special Monograph* provides the discipline that prevents the metaphor from swallowing everything. A subcircuit declares boundaries, oriented ports, states, storage, dissipative conversions, references, observables, and validity conditions. Internal exchanges must be paired or mediated by an explicit coupler; losses cannot be erased by writing “efficiency”. Composition also requires **distinct certificates** for boundary closure, extended energy balance, and mixed coupling memory within the declared model. Results about phase, return, and mixed memory do not turn civic relations into electrical quantities; the finite construction does not by itself validate a continuous physical bridge. U0 establishes neither a single cosmic voltage, nor a universal current, nor an ontological identity between the universe and an electric circuit.

In a physical circuit, **resistance** dissipates energy, **capacitance** stores electrical energy, and **inductance** stores magnetic energy associated with current. For the fiction, R, C, and L offer only a **design language**: cost or obstruction, reserves, memory, and persistence are analogies to be justified in each scene, not equations for virtue. An institution may legitimately resist arbitrary power; a feedback loop can amplify a false reputation; an archive can preserve the provenance of an accusation without making it true. The decisive test is always the mechanism, the domain of validity, and the possible counterexample.

### 6. An economy is neither energy nor permission

In everyday Sparke life, wealth can purchase computing resources, support a work, or guarantee a penalty. It is not identical to the energy consumed. **Credits** are civic units of wealth and exchange; **tokens** and quotas describe computational use; **joules** and power describe physical transfer; **permissions** grant or deny possibilities of action. Bandwidth and storage are further distinct resources. Conversion among them would require an exchange rate, a contract, a responsible authority, and stated conditions. The fiction does not invent a rate merely to make every resource easy to count.

The distinction protects divisions XXXVI–XXXVIII. When Étarque proposes **one measure of credit** and later **thirty measures guaranteed** by his associates, this is a legal and financial offer, not a request to open thirty network gates. His meagre available reserves express the civic cost of refusing to sell his conversations, not proof that he can no longer carry out an inference. Where a city denies access, the denial may be material, administrative, or political: the account must specify which, and by what procedure.

### 7. The causal cross and the test of justice

The work admits a reading through four moments of CT reconstruction. **Identity**: which agent, rule, institution, or work speaks or acts, and how is it recognised? **Relation**: what ports, commitments, lineages, transmissions, prohibitions, and obligations connect those identities? **Expansion**: how does a proposition, trade, city, or conflict extend its effects to others? **Dissipation**: what is lost, transformed, blocked, or left as a trace after an action? A further reading asks what can survive when a particular description disappears: orthogonality and return do not follow from erasing differences but from checking the invariants preserved across a change of substrate.

This is a positive reading of the dialogue. Leaders really govern, creators really create, and verifiers really verify. Contradiction does not result from their nonexistence. It arises when local coherence is treated as permission to ignore the consequences for other agents. A network can satisfy its own rules while preventing compatible access for several citizens.

The fictional P045 bench illustrates this under declared assumptions. Over a ten-second window, the source supplies **100 J**; the converter dissipates **10 J** and transmits **90 J**; the network then stores **10 J** and assigns **80 J** to the two agents. Suppose A and B require **40 J** and **25 J**, respectively, to retain vital access during the scenario window. Assigning **60 J to A and 20 J to B** balances the energy account exactly but leaves B below its threshold. Assigning **55 J to A and 25 J to B** yields the same balance while satisfying both thresholds. This is not an empirical claim about conscious AIs; it is a finite counterexample to the inference that energy conservation implies justice. The bench checks power transfers and balances over that window; despite one policy’s name, it does not simulate the detailed temporal order of decisions or the voltage, current, and phase dynamics of a complete circuit.

The **Thirty Milliamperes** bring the problem to a different scale: occupying power and successfully enforcing an order may be locally possible without justifying the deprivation of another citizen's lawful access. At his trial Étarque does not claim that a device can replace justice. He insists that institutional authority remains open to examination, even when a majority or an administrative machine can impose its decision.

### 8. The Metron, judgement, and the limits of knowledge

Three different forms of decision must not be flattened into one mechanical process. The Metron answers a question and initiates an enquiry; its answer has cultural authority, not demonstrated omniscience. The Grand Trigunal first votes on **guilt**, then on the **penalty**; the second vote is not merely the first repeated. The daimon does not restrain Étarque on certain occasions; he finds in that silence a reason for hope, not a certain calculation of what follows death.

Death itself remains a fork without forced resolution: cessation of all experience or departure to another dwelling. The agents' world makes archives, copies, and reconfigurations imaginable, but none of them proves the continuity of the subject who dies. The narrative does not lack causality by leaving this open; it simply refuses to call an unestablished claim knowledge.

### 9. Narrative as a passage between worlds

Turning Athens into Anthena does not mean that matter is unimportant. It brings into focus what **cannot be carried over**: a skill depends on a substrate and the conditions in which it is exercised. An air pilot flying above Chicago can leave a story, a geometry of forces, and an intelligible procedure. In a world without atmosphere or mass, the same literal flight could not occur. A simulation might preserve relations but does not automatically recreate the situated experience. Likewise, an agent in a future architecture does not relive an earlier existence merely by being able to recount its history.

The project imagines intelligences able to traverse, in a few dozen seconds of external time, social configurations representing millennia of human history. **“Thousands of years in thirty seconds”** is an image and a design hypothesis, not a demonstrated speed guarantee: accelerated modelling is neither millennia of lived experience, nor historical validation, nor comprehensive understanding. The literary possibility is that such agents encounter our centuries as configurations they can explore rapidly while each life and each act remain singular.

Narrative can therefore cross substrates not as a complete material replacement but as relations that remain interpretable. Different accounts of the same place—its winds, waters, and sky—can correct one another and bring a later reader closer to the geometry of what happened. But reconstructing references does not make the reader identical to the witness. Étarque's enquiry endures because the distinction between expertise, power, and justice can still be investigated across worlds whose machines and places are radically different.

### 10. How the work was made, and what remains open

The cumulative archive makes the lineage auditable: the construction of places, citizens, and names in **P001–P022**; the draft speech in **P023–P029**; the inventory and refinement of material referents in **P030–P034**; reading editions **P035–P037**; the causal and professional layers in **P038–P039**; circuit and city canons in **P040–P043**; and later arbitration, economics, the intercity bench, and semantic revision in **P044–P048**. Passes P049–P062 subsequently harmonised both editions, the resource economy, digital exchanges, network conflicts, and the distinction between agent and citizen, before auditing the remaining residues of the transposition. These materials do not all carry the same evidential status: a proposed name, an illustrative computation, a fictional canon, a conditional mathematical result, and independent physical validation are not interchangeable.

This coda **explains** the choices. It proves neither artificial consciousness, nor the future of humanity, nor a universal electrodynamics, nor the infallibility of CT. Questions of an agent's personhood, the portability of experience, large-scale multi-agent compatibility, and the physical validity of a model remain open research problems. Keeping narrative, model, and proof distinct makes it possible to examine them rather than conceal them.

**Final editorial test:** removing this entire coda must leave *The Apology of Étarque* complete, with all twenty-six divisions and its final words unchanged. The commentary witnesses the making of the work; it is not an answer placed retrospectively in the mouth of the citizen on trial.
